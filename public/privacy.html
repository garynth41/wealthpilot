Privacy policy

Last updated November 7, 2026

WealthPilot keeps a household’s budget, bills, accounts and plans in one place. This page explains what the app keeps, why, who can see it, and how to delete it. It covers the website and the iPhone and Android apps — they are the same app and use the same account.

In short

  • Your information is used only to run the app for you and the people in your household.
  • No ads, no tracking, no third-party analytics. Your information is never sold, rented or shared for marketing.
  • You can export everything, and you can delete your account from inside the app at any time.

1. What the app keeps

Your account

Your email address and password. The password is stored only as a one-way scrambled value by the sign-in service, so nobody — including us — can read it. If you turn them on: two-factor sign-in and its recovery codes (kept by the sign-in service), and passkeys you add on the website. A display name, if you or a household owner set one.

What you and your household enter

Accounts and balances, transactions, budgets, bills and payments, income and pay dates, debts, savings goals, investments and tax lots, property and vehicles, insurance policies, credit scores you record, tax details, the estate checklist, notes and comments, and any receipts or statements you attach. Wherever an account or card number appears, only its last four digits are kept.

Household membership

Who belongs to each household, their role (owner, editor or viewer), which sections they may open, and pending invitations (the invited person’s email address and the access offered).

Activity history

Which member added, changed or deleted what, and when — so a household can see its own history. Owners see it under Settings → Activity.

If you connect a bank (optional)

Bank sync works through SimpleFIN Bridge, a separate service you sign up for and connect to your bank yourself. With the connection key you give the app, it brings in your account names, balances and transactions once a day, and when you ask. The key is kept on our servers only — it is never sent to the app or to a browser — and only the last four digits of account numbers are saved.

If you connect email (optional, website only)

On the website you can let the app read bill and statement emails from Gmail or Outlook. Your browser talks to Google or Microsoft directly; the app saves only the details it finds — who the bill is from, the amount, the due date and the last four digits of the account. The email itself is never stored, and the sign-in to your mailbox stays in your browser. The phone apps do not offer this.

Alerts and reminders (optional)

The alerts you choose and, for email alerts, the address they go to. For notifications in a browser, a push subscription for that browser. On phones, bill reminders are scheduled on the phone itself and do not pass through our servers.

Error reports

When something goes wrong, the app records a short technical report — the error, the page, the app version and the type of device or browser — with your user ID, so the problem can be found and fixed.

Kept on your device

Your sign-in session; a read-only copy of your household so the app can open without a connection (you can turn this off under Settings → This device, and it is removed when you sign out); and small preferences such as the theme and whether reminders are on.

What the app does not collect: your location, contacts, photos you have not attached, browsing history or advertising identifiers. There are no third-party analytics, tracking, advertising or social-media tools in the app or the website — the only technical information kept is the error reports above, used only to fix problems.

2. How it is used

Only to provide the app: to show and calculate your household’s numbers, sync your bank if you connected one, send the alerts and reminders you turned on, keep nightly backups, keep accounts secure, and find and fix problems. It is not used for advertising, not sold, and not used to build profiles about you. Nobody looks at a household’s information except when you ask for help and it is needed, or when it is necessary to keep the service running and secure.

For people in the European Economic Area and the UK: the legal basis is that the processing is needed to provide the service you asked for, and our legitimate interest in keeping it secure and working.

4. How it is protected

  • Everything is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and encrypted at rest by the database provider.
  • The database itself checks every request: a household’s information is returned only to its members, and only the sections each member may open.
  • Bank connection keys are stored apart from everything else and are never sent to any app or browser.
  • Two-factor sign-in, recovery codes, passkeys on the website, signing out other devices, an idle lock (with Face ID or fingerprint on phones), and a switch that hides amounts on screen.
  • Nightly backups are stored privately; only the household’s members can download them.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your information, you will be told as the law requires.

5. How long it is kept, and deleting it

  • Your information stays until you delete it, delete your household or delete your account.
  • Delete your account any time in the app or on the website: Settings → Security → Delete my account. It happens immediately. Households that only you belong to are deleted with everything in them, including attached files and backups. Households you share stay with the other members, and your name in their history becomes “a former member”. Step-by-step instructions.
  • Delete a household (owners): Settings → General → Delete this household. It is read-only for 7 days, so any owner can change their mind, and is then removed for good with its files and backups.
  • Nightly backups are deleted after 30 days. Error reports are deleted after 90 days.
  • After a household is deleted, a small record that it existed is kept — its name, when it was deleted and how many files were removed — with none of its financial information.
  • The service providers above keep routine security logs (for example sign-in times and IP addresses) for a limited time under their own policies.

6. Your choices and rights

  • See and export everything: Settings → Backup & data (a complete backup file, or transactions as a spreadsheet).
  • Correct anything by editing it in the app.
  • Delete as described above.
  • Turn off alerts, phone reminders, the bank connection, the email connection and the offline copy at any time.

Depending on where you live — for example California, the European Economic Area or the UK — you may have the right to access, correct, delete or take a copy of your information, to object to or limit its use, and to complain to your data protection authority. Use the tools above or contact us; you will not be treated differently for using these rights. WealthPilot does not sell or share personal information as those laws define it.

7. Children

WealthPilot is meant for adults managing a household’s money. It is not directed to children under 13 (16 in some countries), and information from children is not knowingly collected. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and it will be deleted.

8. Changes

When this policy changes, this page shows the new date. For significant changes, the app will tell you before they take effect.
